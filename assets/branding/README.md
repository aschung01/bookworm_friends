# Libstack icon pipeline

`app_icon_source.png` is the **source of truth** — the chalk artwork, exactly as
generated, on its original background. Currently `m4-single-book`: one book,
spine and bookmark, no stack. Everything else in this directory is
derived, as is everything under `ios/`, `android/`, `web/`, `macos/` and
`windows/`. Never hand-edit generated files.

## Regenerating

```sh
./assets/branding/build_icons.sh
```

Creates `.venv/` with Pillow on first run. The script builds the masters, runs
`flutter_launcher_icons`, then patches two things the generator gets wrong.

Afterwards, eyeball the two previews before committing:

| Preview             | Checks                                                                                |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `_mask_preview.png` | Android adaptive foreground under the circle and squircle launcher masks              |
| `_dark_preview.png` | iOS 18 dark and tinted appearances (white-on-transparent looks blank in most viewers) |

## The masters

All five are generated by `build_master.py`:

| File                      | Purpose                                  | Notes                                                                                           |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `app_icon.png`            | iOS light, Windows, web, store listings  | Full bleed, opaque. No alpha — the App Store rejects it.                                        |
| `app_icon_mark.png`       | iOS 18 dark + tinted                     | White mark on transparency.                                                                     |
| `app_icon_foreground.png` | Android adaptive foreground + monochrome | Pre-inset to 80% for the launcher mask.                                                         |
| `app_icon_maskable.png`   | web manifest `purpose: maskable`         | Opaque, inset. Maskable icons may be cropped to a circle of 80% of the canvas.                  |
| `app_icon_macos.png`      | macOS                                    | Rounded 824/1024 body, transparent margins, per Apple's grid. macOS applies no mask of its own. |

### Why alpha is derived, not chroma-keyed

The artwork's background is not one flat colour — it ranges over roughly
`(35..52, 179..186, 134..146)` — and chalk edges are deliberately soft. Keying
on a single green would leave a dirty fringe around every stroke and would hard-
clip the soft edges.

Instead, alpha comes from **how far each pixel has travelled from the background
green toward white**, taking the per-channel maximum. Soft edges stay soft, and
the mark can then be re-composited onto an exact `#09BC8A` plate. This is also
what makes the Android monochrome layer work: that layer is tinted purely by
alpha, so a keyed matte with fringing would look filthy under a themed icon.

### Why alpha is then gated by distance from a stroke

Deriving alpha from brightness alone also lifts any **chalk dust on the board**.
Artwork drawn on a smudged plate rather than a clean one carries a faint haze
well above the noise floor: M4 put ~16% of the canvas into alpha 26–49, which is
invisible on the green plate but composites as a grey halo around the mark on the
iOS dark and tinted layers.

Dust and the genuine soft chalk feather overlap in alpha _level_, so no threshold
separates them. They do not overlap in **distance from a stroke** — a feather
hugs one by definition, haze sits out in open plate. So `gate_to_mark()` dilates
the confident core (alpha ≥ 128) by 8px and masks the derived alpha with it. On
artwork with a clean background this is a no-op.

This was a latent bug, not an M4 one: K4B's ungated alpha bbox was also very
nearly the full canvas, so the icon shipped before M4 carried a fainter version
of the same halo. The gate removes ~20% of K4B's alpha and ~31% of M4's, and in
both cases leaves the opaque core (alpha ≥ 128) bit-for-bit identical — it only
ever discards sub-threshold haze, never drawing.

### Why `adaptive_icon_foreground_inset: 0`

The safe-zone inset is baked into `app_icon_foreground.png` rather than
delegated to the generator, so it can be _verified_ against a real circle mask
(`_mask_preview.png`) instead of guessed. An adaptive layer is 108dp but only
the central ~72dp survives masking.

## What `build_icons.sh` patches after generation

1. The generator copies the full-bleed icon into the manifest's
   `purpose: maskable` slots, where the mark would be cropped. Overwritten with
   `app_icon_maskable.png`.
2. It emits no favicon. `web/favicon.png` is rendered at 32px.

## Changing the artwork

Replace `app_icon_source.png` and re-run the build. The script assumes:

- square, ideally 1024×1024 (anything else is resampled)
- a **white or near-white mark** on a **mid-tone brand-green background**
- at least a 12px clean border, which is sampled to estimate the background

A mark that is not white, or a background that is not clearly darker than the
mark, needs `extract_alpha()` in `build_master.py` adjusted.

After any swap, check the numbers as well as the previews:

- `mark coverage` printed by the build — M4 is 25.2% of canvas
- the alpha bbox should hug the drawing, not the whole canvas (a bbox near
  1024² means background haze is leaking through the dust gate)
- the Android foreground's furthest opaque pixel from centre must stay inside
  r=338, the 66%-diameter safe circle — M4 is 312

## Design constraints

Anything replacing this mark has to satisfy:

- **Legible at 48px first.** Design there, then scale up.
- **Brand green cannot carry contrast on white** — it scores 2.45:1, see
  `lib/constants/app_theme.dart`. Green plate, white mark, never the reverse.
- **Prefer a landmark over a texture.** Fine detail becomes a scratch under
  downscaling; a distinct shape survives.
- **It must survive being flattened to pure alpha**, for the Android monochrome
  layer.

Exploration and rejected directions: `docs/logo-review/` (open `index.html`).
