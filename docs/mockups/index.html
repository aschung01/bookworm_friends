Design mockups

Self-contained pages — no build, no server, no network. Open any of them straight from disk. Each is data-driven: screens are declared once and drawn by one renderer, so a changed decision cannot leave half the set stale.

Open questions

Reading streaks — the bookmark, the card, the list superseded in part · ribbon drawn wrong, page_count answered →
A streak needs a daily signal and the schema has none. Books carry a start_date and a finish_date, memos and praise carry a created_at, and no row anywhere means “I read today”. Worse, the corpus shows the opposite behaviour: 168 of 293 finished books have start_date == finish_date, which is why bookReadingSpanDays has to treat a same-day pair as unknown — people catalogue, they do not track. So the proposal is not a button but a bookmark you move, paying for four things with one gesture: the daily signal, a progress percentage the app cannot currently show at all, a real reading span, and the first input the poke_events feed has ever had — its own migration defers the feed because there is “no activity to show”. Three anatomies, and the axis is resolution, computed rather than asserted: hanging from the chrome gives 2.5pt of travel per page but its tail crosses the tab strip and lies over the Notes list; on the cover, 0.56; across the fore-edge, 0.33, the truest metaphor and the only exactly proportional one, needing 32pt of headroom the hero's 20pt padding does not have. Drawing it caught the ribbon's tip landing at 53% of a cover the band beside it labelled 46% — two indicators of one fact disagreeing — and the 8pt nub is the fix. The streak's record is the due-date card from a library book's back pocket, not a flame, which is the visual language card-seal already established; a missed day is pale ink and never a red cross, because a second scolding is what makes people log days they did not read and put fiction into LibraryCardStats. Friends take Duolingo's unranked Friend Streaks pattern and explicitly reject its League: pages are not commensurable between two readers, so a pages leaderboard pays people to pick easier books, while days are. Open, and load-bearing: page_count is nullable and nobody has asked the corpus how often — if it is most books, the fallback button is the feature and the ribbon is a bonus, which inverts the design; a reader between books has no ribbon to move and loses a streak having done nothing wrong; a backwards drag has three readings and no chosen one; and the friend row's trailing slot is already spoken for by FriendReading.finishedCount.
Read pile — the type on a spine open · type only, no geometry →
The most bookish surface in the app is set in a UI token. BookVertical uses AppTextStyles.label, whose own doc says “tab labels, shelf tabs, badges, capsules, buttons” — so in the collapsed Library tab a book title is the same family and weight as the Library / Friends / Card labels 40pt below it, and it carries FontFeature.tabularFigures(), which is right for a count and wrong for a title. The design record here says “the 12pt rotated title” twice; the code ships 13, because the token moved and the spine came along. Three more findings, each measured off the drawing rather than asserted: 30 of the 110–124pt spine is padding, spent clearing an arch 5pt deep, so the measure is 80–94pt where it could be 93–107; half the truncation is not about measure at all but the parenthetical original title a Korean catalogue supplies as a matter of course (그로스 해킹(Growth Hacking) fits whole once cut at the bracket); and Center hides the per-book jitter this feature was built to add, because it puts every title at the middle of its own spine. On the face: the serif is unavailable rather than unchosen — AppFonts.serif is subset to app_ko.arb, so 17 syllables across ten real titles fall through and fontFamilyFallback changes family mid-title; the page renders both failure modes. And designhouseOTFLight.otf already ships, carries all 11,172 Hangul syllables, and is referenced nowhere — 376KB that never draws a glyph. Five layouts × twelve faces × nine sizes, switchable and independent — including five Korean serifs built as mockup-only subsets, among them Gowun Batang whole, so you can see what the app’s own serif would look like if it were not cut. Truncation is counted live off the drawing: 9 of 10 titles at now, 4 of 10 once the measure is fixed. Renders to PNG via python3 docs/mockups/spine-type/render.py, which also prints the serif’s per-title coverage.
Library Card — the seal behind the shelf open · caused by the fix above →
The new two-board shelf stands in front of the embossed seal — and the seal is what Candlelight exists to reveal. sealOpacity is 0.1 in daylight, a shape in the stock rather than a mark on it, and 1.0 under the flame; it was drawn faint on purpose so the candle would have something real to find. So this is not an ornament being covered, it is the card’s one delight feature. The measurement is narrower than it first looks: nothing reaches the seal while the shelf needs one board, because a one-board shelf sits at the bottom of the well — so the corner is intact to the 17th book overlapping and the 20th as spines, then loses a third of the disc in one step when the second board fills. Side by side never reaches it at all. Six placements, each costed: now (books in front), balance (split the shelf evenly, which fixes the whole common range for no capacity at all but not a full card), reserve (hold the corner; the only one that holds at every count, at −3 overlapping, −4 spines, −1 side by side, and free below eleven books), body (move it below the perforation, where an emboss belongs, into slack whose size depends on how much the reader has read), watermark and adaptive, the last two kept as the record of why they were refused. The hatch on each drawing is the actual intersection of disc and books, not a description of one. Verify with node docs/mockups/card-seal/verify.js.
Library Card — the cover row crop shipped bug · decided · built →
The cover row crops every jacket it draws, and the approved drawing could not show it. _tierFor hands out boxes at 0.36 and 0.50 where kDefaultCoverAspect is 0.67, and BoxFit.cover resolves the disagreement by centre-cropping: at twelve books each cover is 17pt wide in a 31pt jacket, so 20.7pt of artwork per cover is discarded, half off each side. The jitter makes it worse rather than softer — it perturbs width and height independently, so the aspect wanders between 0.30 and 0.49 and every cover is cropped by a different amount, which is why the row reads as a rendering fault. It shipped because artCoverRow in library-card-share fills each cover with a flat swatch, and a flat 0.36 rectangle reads as a spine. Ten options drawn against the real geometry, from a one-line alignment stopgap upward. Decided: shelf — two boards, three fixed sizes, and nothing squeezed. What boards and phase differed in turned out to be one choice rather than two designs — whether a book you have read is drawn as a cover or as a spine — so it was handed to the reader as a setting: a panel at the card’s top right offering Covers or Spines. Then it was taken back. The card draws overlapping covers and nothing else: a shelf of spines is a picture of a different object, and covers are the one asset a competitor cannot fake, so a setting that hides them is a setting that gives away the point. The withdrawn modes stay drawable there as the evidence, relabelled rather than deleted. A cover is 52.8pt, a spine is 16.6pt (BookVertical’s own proportion at that height), and a leaning cover advances by 16.6pt — equal to the spine width, because a leaning book shows exactly a spine’s worth of itself. Currently-reading books sit at the front as whole covers with a bookmark and no month stamp. Past capacity nothing thins or crowds; the shelf prints how many it is not showing — so capacity becomes a fact rather than something to engineer: 31 covers, 26 with two books open, 24 with three — three fewer than before the seal’s corner was held, with the withdrawn modes measured beside them at 9 and 31. Three is also as many books as can be open at the front, because the top board must still be able to draw a finished one once the seal is held: that is kCardReadingMax, and it was two until the rule stopped being a fraction of the board and became the guarantee. Two boards is also the last layout the well permits (a third leaves 30.7pt of cover against a 51.9pt floor). Resolved: the bookmark carries the distinction and the figure does not move — the hero keeps counting finished books, so a card with two open books prints 22 over 24 covers, and the two wearing a bookmark are visibly a different class of thing. A figure counting both would make “22 books” mean “20 finished and 2 started”, which is a worse number and a claim library_card_stats.dart does not make. Resolved: there is no setting, so nothing is baked into the PNG and there is no preference to store, migrate or explain — the panel, its stored preference, the export field, the analytics dimension and the spine renderer went with it. boards and phase are kept browsable as the squeezing they replace. Every cover on the page carries a title, which is the one thing the original drawing did not do. Verify with node docs/mockups/card-cover-crop/verify.js. Now built: the geometry is a pure function in card_shelf_plan.dart and card_cover_row.dart only paints it, guarded by card_shelf_plan_test.dart (41) and card_cover_row_test.dart (25) — the latter asserting the box aspect directly at every count, because the defect was invisible to a test that asserted the size the code meant to draw. Render the result with flutter test test/card_shelf_render_preview.dart, which draws jackets with content in them for the same reason this page does. The panel is built too, in card_display_panel.dart, and the setting is stored on the device — card_display_provider.dart, beside the app’s two other display preferences. Not a profiles column: nobody but the reader ever reads it, because the card a friend looks at is rendered by their app. The last cover layout is kept under its own key so Covers returns the reader to their own layout across a restart, and destination_row_test.dart asserts the end of the chain that matters: the setting is in the CardExportRequest, and therefore in the bytes.
Shelf overflow — when a shelf holds more books than its row can show 3 options · 3 open →
A shelf is one horizontal ListView, and on a 390pt phone only 3.58 books fit in it — measured on the drawing, not asserted: bookHeight is screenHeight * 0.15 (126.6), the cover is 2/3 of that (84.4), and the row is screenWidth * 0.95 less 15pt each side. So a twelve-book shelf shows three and a sliver, and the only thing saying otherwise is a clip through the fourth cover — which reads as a rendering fault rather than as an invitation to swipe.

A signals the scroll (count on the name tab, fade at the edge): cheap, additive, fixes legibility but not reach. B wraps onto as many planks as it takes — drawn so its cost is visible rather than argued about, at roughly 1,100pt of scroll for two shelves. C caps the wrap at two planks and sends the remainder to a +6 chip and a shelf page.

Drawing them surfaced three costs that prose had missed: wrapping breaks the shelfHeroTag(shelf.id) contract (N heroes, one tag, one route — Flutter throws); edit mode’s horizontal ReorderableListView has no wrapping equivalent; and book width depends on a decoded cover aspect, so a wrap computed from measured width reflows as images load. The frames also caught something argument could not: both wrap options push a plank behind the read sheet.
Friends & invites — mutual friendship, invite-only friends-only decided · 2 open →
Replacing the one-directional follows row with mutual friendship, where invite links are the only path in. Checked against Flighty’s real flows on Mobbin, which changed the design twice.

There is no request queue and no badge, because the queue was never created by mutual friendship — it was created by handle search, and handle search is gone. That also deletes status, requested_by, the UPDATE policy an accept would need, two RPCs and the four-state button. And since a link tap needs no delivery, nothing here is blocked on the FCM v1 migration — a request queue would have been.

Accepting is a full-screen consent moment with no Decline button: the ✕ is the refusal. Its mark is two libraries interleaving rather than two portraits — Flighty joins two faces with a flight path because a flight is what they share, and a stack is what Libstack shares.

Looking at Flighty’s sheets properly changed two more things. Their invite sheet shows no link and no code — it shows a globe carrying three friends’ flights with live status pills, then a second paragraph that is purely emotional. It sells the payoff. Ours now shows three shelves with the status this app reports. And they confirm a new friendship on its own screen, using that moment to offer per-friend alerts — a screen I had missed entirely, and the best possible introduction to Manage friend.

Drawn in the app’s own materials — Pretendard and GowunBatang loaded off disk from assets/fonts/, every size a real token from app_text_styles.dart scaled to the frame. Chrome is post-redesign: the Friends sheet is the switcher, a visit is ✕ / title / Poke with no tab bar, and Manage friend hangs off the long press the deleted FriendRail used to own.

Decided: friends-only. public-profiles is kept as the record of the alternative, and is the only version where UserLibraryPage still has a way to be reached — deleting it is the largest simplification available here. Thirteen screens, four flows.
Friend paging — the horizontal pager 4 branches · switcher-drilldown built →
Two reported defects in the shell’s PageView, drawn frame by frame, with the fixes as branches you can diff. 1. A sideways drag on your own library starts a visit. The pager is the root of home_page.dart and its physics is gated on mode == LibraryMode.library alone, so nothing about the Library or Card tab makes the horizontal axis dead. It leaks in through three surfaces, all from one formula: ScrollPhysics.shouldAcceptUserOffset is pixels != 0 || min != max, and applyNewDimensions feeds it to setCanDrag() — so a shelf whose books fit, a read pile that does not overflow, and the whole Card tab all decline the drag and hand it to the pager. 2. Tapping a friend three rows down sweeps the two in between. _syncPageToFriend calls animateToPage, and onPageChanged is a position report fired for every page crossed — so each intermediate _FriendLibraryPage mounts, fires userLibraryProvider and userFinishedBooksProvider (both autoDispose.family, so both real Supabase round trips), draws a spinner and unmounts: 2(k−1) queries thrown away to show one friend. The frame that explains why bug 1 feels broken: onPageChanged fires at page 0.50, not on settle, so the tab bar is destroyed, the rail’s row is inserted and _barExtent shoves your own shelves down 56pt while they are still half on screen. The same mid-flight frame between two friends is fine, and is drawn beside it — nothing about the chrome changes across that boundary, which is the argument for acting on the self/friend boundary alone. Four branches, and the last one is the recommendation. visit-scoped is the smallest and is two lines: scope the axis to a visit through a _pagerLive latch, and jumpToPage for a non-adjacent tap. It has to be a latch, not a read of selectedFriend — changing physics mid-drag rebuilds the ScrollPosition and would cancel the return swipe at its midpoint. pager-in-visit is the structural alternative: friends-only pager, mounted over your library for the duration of a visit, which makes the boundary uncrossable rather than gated and is the only shape that can cross-fade. sheet-switcher goes further and deletes the rail and the swipe outright, making the Friends sheet the one switcher and keeping it reachable inside a visit. Not because of the follow counts: an earlier draft priced the rail against the Phase 4 audit's 37 follow rows, and that reading is void — the app was pulled from the store years ago, so those rows are a dormant corpus, not readers who declined to follow anybody. What argues for it is the gesture and the form factor: friends_sheet.dart already calls a strip of nameless avatars “worse than the list itself” while the shell ships exactly that at the top of the screen, and a named, scrollable list is what scales when the relaunch has a real follow graph. It costs the tab bar coming back inside a visit (answerable: libraryTabProvider documents tabs as sheet selectors, not places), a meaning for Card (hers, share suppressed, three items kept because the native CNTabBar should not change shape), and a caching fix that is a prerequisite rather than a follow-up. Two numbers came out of drawing it rather than out of prose: the collapsed Friends sheet yields 1.6 rows at the shared 20% floor, so the switcher needs a collapsedBody like the read sheet's pile; and header + three named rows + the bar's reserve is ~46% of the screen, against the rail's 48pt — names are not free, and the sheet wins on legibility rather than on area. Bringing the tab bar back into a visit forces three further answers, and the branch shipped with the first one wrong until it was drawn. Closing a visit: the rail's glass × moves into the bar's leading slot — the exit cannot live in the Friends list's head row, because a visit has three tabs and that row is on one of them, which left the Library and Card tabs as states with no visible way out. _LibraryBar keeps “actions out of the slot where users expect Cancel”, which reserves that slot for a dismissal rather than forbidding one. Her reading history: restoring the bar's 78pt reserve takes that off her expanded month grid — available = maxExtent − bottomReserve − viewPadding.bottom, and _FriendLibraryPage passes 0 today — so roughly one row of covers, which is exactly what decided.html counted as the thing a visit buys. What a tab tap does: two answers, drawn as two branches. sheet-switcher lets Library and Card re-scope to the friend; switcher-drilldown never re-scopes any tab, so tapping Library or Card ends the visit and her reads become a second level of the Friends sheet — with no back control in it, because a second tap on the lit Friends tab is what comes back up. The first keeps one-tap switching and gets her card for free; the second gets a tab bar that cannot lie and three visible exits, and pays two taps per switch. switcher-drilldown is the one chosen, on the simpler rule — and it is also the cheaper of the two, which this page had backwards at first: it owes no My Library head row, no Friends collapsedBody and no friend-facing card, and replaces them with a single enum provider. docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-22-friend-navigation-plan.md implements it, and is now marked implemented — with the number the drawings could not give: her band and yours are both 320pt on a 375×667 phone, identical because her read view carries no chrome yours does not. A ‹ Friends label was built for that header first and cut: it cost 16pt of library and displaced her year filter to fit. Still open across the branches: whether your library belongs on the axis at all, how the collapsed switcher gets its height, whether a tab tap is an acceptable way to leave, and whether the friend read filter should stay shared across friends now that a live tab bar can make it appear to change on its own. Chrome, shelves and sheets are the same renderer library-shell/decided.html uses, so they cannot drift from the decided design. Verify with node docs/mockups/friend-paging/verify.js.

Typography deciding →
Which face the app should set, and what it is setting today without having chosen to. AppTheme._build never sets textTheme, so all 88 hardcoded TextStyles merge onto Material 3's Roboto metrics by way of bodyMedium — letterSpacing 0.25 and height 1.43 at every size, black87/pure white instead of the primaryText tokens, and CupertinoSystemText (SF Pro Text, the optical size for ≤19pt) on the Card's 46pt figure, which is why StatTile has to hand-tune letterSpacing: -0.02 * figureSize1px = 1pt on a 402×874 frame, and five serif display faces on the Library Card hero, which is the only type large enough to justify a second family. Five switches — locale, metrics, scale, display face, secondaryText — so a face is judged against the others under identical conditions, and so the metrics question can be answered before the font question. UI face and display face are separate lists on purpose: collapsing them into one list of "candidates" drew three frames identically, since all three shared Pretendard for UI and differed only in a face the current scale never reaches. Records that Hangul is the deciding constraint (SF Pro has none, so 소설 is Apple SD Gothic Neo today — a second face with a second x-height), that the 18pt page title and the 18pt sheet title are the same size so the sheet does not read as the foreground, that secondaryText is 2.07:1 on white and so is why the empty state is nearly invisible, and — measured in the page rather than asserted — that Noto Sans KR, IBM Plex Sans KR and Gothic A1 already ship fixed-width digits, so the tabularFigures() fix is a no-op for three of the ten faces and the recommendation is coupled to the face. Also records that a display face is far cheaper than it looks: scoped to what it actually sets — digits and two fixed l10n strings — every serif here lands at 4–8KB per weight against 1.4–13.4MB unscoped, which dissolved an earlier version's cost argument entirely. And that Latin coverage and Latin quality disagree: Gowun Batang has the best coverage of the five serifs (Latin-1 100%, Ext-A 99%, against Song Myung's 5%/0%) but the lightest bold — its w700 stem measures 250, thinner than Hahmlet's w400 — so the Card figure gets its own three-way block, where a split face (serif title, Pretendard w800 figure) is the recommendation. Switch with L M S D C. The faces are subset to this page's glyphs and inlined by build-fonts.py, so it still opens from disk with no network — which matters more here than elsewhere, since offline a CDN version would not fail, it would draw all seven candidates in one fallback face and answer wrongly.
