Self-contained pages — no build, no server, no network. Open any of them straight from disk. Each is data-driven: screens are declared once and drawn by one renderer, so a changed decision cannot leave half the set stale.

Open questions

Where to read this — opening a book in Kindle, Apple Books, Play Books or Libby decided: bar-sheet · 2 superseded · 4 open →
Decided: an app-bar icon opening a sheet. It costs the hero and the tab body nothing, resolves availability where a spinner is already unremarkable, and is the only option reachable from both tabs because the bar is pinned by SliverPersistentHeader. The two it beat are kept browsable as superseded branches, since what rejected them is the useful part.

The two intents have inverted reliability, and that is the whole design problem. Of six cells in the store × intent matrix, only two are exact per-book links: Play Books for both, Apple Books for buying. Amazon publishes no per-book deep link at all, so Kindle can only ever be a search, and kindle:// opens the app’s library rather than a title.

So a row’s second line is load-bearing rather than decorative, and the verb carries the capability: Read in Play Books promises a book, Open Kindle promises an app. Drawing the chips layout is what settled the first point — a chip has no second line, so Kindle can be marked but never explained. Both are asserted rather than left to a copy review.

Ownership is unknowable — canLaunchUrl proves an app is installed, never that the book is in it — so the design infers it from a tap and the sheet collapses to Your copy afterwards. Status and isSelf conditionality then fall out for free instead of needing rules like _ReactButton’s status != 2. Costs one nullable column, in the shape book_cover_color.sql already uses.

Still open: a tap is not a purchase; whether the glyph should change when a store is known (copy cannot substitute — the tooltip is equally true either way); text labels or brand marks; and the ko store set, which is a correctness problem rather than a wording one — Libby maps to US and UK library systems and Korean readers use 리디북스, 밀리의서재, 예스24 and 교보.

Empty-state art — five vector families, three chalk treatments, and the generated chalk art 14 versions · 7 screens · shipped →
An inventory of every moment the product comes up empty-handed, and three candidate art families. Today: sadCharacter.svg covers both library empty states in hardcoded #ADB5BD — the exact grey secondaryText was demoted from for washing out — with a literal-white face that glows on the dark theme, and a frown aimed at a brand-new reader the copy is trying to invite in. The add-book sheet uses Icons.menu_book_outlined at 100pt and the Notes tab Icons.note_outlined at 48 — stock glyphs doing illustration duty. The search misses and the scan dead-end are text only.

Four branches, one per identity position. worm-family re-inks the pre-rename mascot with AppColors tokens — kept honest with a caveat, since the logo review retired the worm (“that silhouette is Bugdroid”) and the shipping icon is now m4-single-book. stack-family speaks that icon's own language: the p1 offset back board, the ribbon cut in as a notch, the s3j fanned stack, a dashed board as “the book that isn't here yet / wasn't found”. line-family is the quiet floor: bespoke 3.5px-stroke glyphs in the register the Material icons occupy today, one brand accent each. chalk-family is the icon's mark in its own colours: a white (surface) chalk mass with the hand contour in ink and the green ribbon — which is how the icon's knockout actually reads — so the page plays the plate and nothing looks like an app icon dropped into the UI. On the dark theme the light mass on the dark page literally becomes chalk on a board. Wobble baked into the paths, no SVG filters, so flutter_svg renders it as-is. Three further treatments branch off chalk-family and change only how the edge is drawn, converging on the icon's actual hand: chalk/dry-line replaces the ruled contour with a breathing dry stroke (per-segment widths, a tilt fringe, edge grain), chalk/grain-edge drops the contour entirely — torn silhouette, ghost line, speckle band, the icon-literal reading — and chalk/torn-soft keeps one thin torn contour as the quiet middle. solid-mass abandons line work altogether for the icon's literal read: one bold solid silhouette — spine slab, lip over the top, ribbon cut in — in secondaryText ink with on-mass details knocked out in surface colour; the construction that actually survives at 48px.

Then vectors stop being the only option. The icon is an AI-generated raster (ai-moodboard/m4-single-book.png), so its chalk grain cannot be hand-written as SVG — but it can be transferred. scripts/chalkify_empty_state_art.py converts each cmass-* vector into the icon's own polarity (white masses on its green plate, ribbon knocked out) and runs it through Bedrock stable-style-transfer with the icon as style image: our composition, the icon's real hand. Polarity is the whole trick — feeding grey-on-white art in makes the model invert the drawing. bedrock-raster shows the results as generated, which immediately reports that a baked green plate reads as an app icon dropped into the UI; bedrock-ink keys the plate out (luminance to alpha) and re-tints to secondaryText, the only form that could ship. Two upstream defects are visible and noted per screen: the ribbon reads as a crown, and green details get knocked out to the plate and vanish. The measured finding: chalk texture dies at 34–48pt, so raster belongs on the 100pt heroes and vectors on the spots.

Then the seeding assumption turns out to be the problem. Style-transfer needs one of our vectors as its content, so output quality is capped by that vector's geometry — it faithfully reproduced the crown-shaped ribbon and deleted every green detail. The three grok-* versions stop seeding: scripts/gen_empty_state_art.py asks xAI Grok Imagine to draw a new illustration with the app icon supplied only as a texture reference, explicitly told to ignore its colours. That is the first art a reviewer accepted as matching the icon. grok-plate shows it as generated, still on the green plate. grok-cutout regenerates on a flat white field — drawn that way so it can be keyed — and scripts/cutout_bg.py strips it to real RGBA, necessary because the xAI endpoint returns JPEG, which has no alpha channel. That version meets every stated constraint but needs one file per theme, and the two independent generations drifted into different drawings. grok-stencil resolves it: scripts/png_probe.py measured the cutouts as ~1% partial alpha over a fully opaque mass, so a srcIn tint would have flattened the grain; scripts/alpha_from_luma.py therefore bakes chalk coverage into alpha, and one file per piece then tints per theme with the texture intact — six assets instead of twelve, and light/dark cannot drift because they are the same geometry.

Shipped. Cut to assets/icons/empty/ with 2x/3x density variants (scripts/cut_empty_state_assets.py) and wired at all seven call sites through one EmptyStateArt widget, replacing sadCharacter.svg and the two Material glyphs. Drawing the states also answered the open question about the combined search miss: it gets a spot, but only when both scopes came up empty. Still open — surfaced by the worm caveat — whether the auth-page hero keeps the retired mascot. Rendered samples in empty-states/art/, generated rasters and their prompts/costs in empty-states/art/gen/ (see manifest.json) and empty-states/PROMPTS.md.
Library search — finding a book you already have 3 versions · 1 superseded by its own drawing →
The orb reaches the catalogue only. Nothing in the app searches the reader’s own library, and the Add Book grid has no idea what they already own — nothing in add_to_library_sheet.dart or LibraryActions.addBook checks, so tapping a book you shelved months ago writes a second row.

The first draft is in here as a superseded version, and drawing it is what killed it. A filter field in the read sheet’s expanded header looked right — a text filter is the same job as the year rail, so it belongs beside it, and calling it a filter rather than search avoided a second magnifying glass. But that surface is finishedBooksProvider, i.e. .eq('status', bookStatusFinished). Of a reader’s three 한강 books it can see one; the other two are drawn on the shelves behind the very sheet the field sits in, because withoutFinishedBooks strips only status 2. verify.js pins exactly that: one cover in the month grid, nine unreachable ones behind it.

Interested is the status the feature is for, and it is the biggest group — book_status_badge.dart measured 133 of 472 books at it. You remember what you read; you forget what you meant to read. main puts one field in the orb with two sections ordered by scope, so ordering replaces a mode and the app keeps exactly one tappable magnifier. Duplicates are tiered by match confidence: an identical ISBN is dropped from the catalogue section, a title-and-author match is kept and tagged — because hiding a book the reader wanted has no recourse, while showing one costs a cover they ignore. Reading streaks — the bookmark, the card, the list spec written · collapsed to 1 version · merged into the shipped status sheet →
Current proposal: stamp-a — the tap keeps meaning “open the book”, and the day is stamped by a one-word chip on the reading-period card. Field research (Fable, Goodreads, Life Reset via Mobbin) showed nobody ships the bookmark as the input device, which is why the six drag versions never satisfied. What replaced them was still too much — a sheet with chips, a wheel, a note and a checkbox — so tap-target cuts it to one wheel for the position (no delta chips, and no Page/Percent segment: page if page_count exists, percent if not, which is two books in three) and draws the day's gesture as an open question with five answers: tap opens the book and the stamp is a button on it (A); tap the ribbon (B, whose 44pt target is 52% of an 84.4pt cover); long-press for a two-item menu (C); one pill for the whole row, naming no book (D); or the cover's own tap (E, which makes one row behave unlike every other row). A won — it invents no gesture and can become B or C later, where E cannot be walked back — and stamp-a then drew A's five possible homes against the real chrome, measuring each: a full-width band button costs 58pt off the top of both tabs, the period card wraps for 32pt, and the same chip cut to “Today” costs 2pt and stays on one line beside the date range. The app bar is free but icon-only next to delete; the FAB is already the Notes composer's; and making the streak chip the target repeats B's target problem. And the reader's own count now has somewhere to live: it always had a numeric home on the Library Card (big figure plus the month's stamp grid), but the library screen carried only states — lamp, tick, moved ribbon — so streak-home draws every slot that could hold it and proposes a ▣ 12 chip in the library bar, grey until today is stamped and green after, which costs no vertical space and points at the Card rather than becoming a second home for the number. It also draws the collision that follows: the Card's hero figure is already finishedCount, so days-in-a-row and books-read end up as two identical brand-green numbers — one of them has to be smaller, and books read should stay the hero. And streak-counter draws the streak the way Duolingo actually builds one — not a number but six moments: the counter in chrome (four states: green recorded, grey open, amber late, blue frozen), the celebration on increment, the milestone, the evening warning, the freeze, and the break. Translated rather than copied: no flame, because the app already owns the ink stamp, the lamp and the wax seal. The warning is amber and states the 4am deadline instead of threatening; the freeze draws a forgiven day differently from a read one; and the break shows the record, not a zero, with an honest repair (“I read Tuesday — fix it”) rather than a purchase. The same version settles the page wheel: a page wheel is fine at 320 stops and absurd at 912 (~91 flicks), so the wheel spins percent — 101 stops for every book ever printed — with the page as a derived label underneath (≈ p.429 of 912). That is also the unit the app ought to store, so the unit segment, the no-page-count fallback and the resolution cliff all disappear at once. The stamp itself is now specified in el-stamp-write: one row per reader per day, upserted, on a 4am rollover, with the streak derived rather than stored — and it deliberately touches neither the position nor start_date. Position becomes a fraction, drawn as each shelf ribbon's horizontal offset so every row shows how far along its book is — and that needs a new column, because books.position turns out to be the shelf ordering index (max 25 across all 473 books, duplicated within a shelf), so there is today nowhere to put a reading position at all. All eight earlier versions stay browsable. And streak-flows settles what a recorded day must remember. Going back to the reference showed that Duolingo's streak is mostly a screen, and that its calendar joins consecutive days into one continuous capsule rather than drawing them as separate marks — a detail this set had missed for twelve versions. Thirty isolated stamps read as confetti; a ligature reads as a thread you would not want to cut, and a gap in it is visibly a cut. Once the run is one object, the only question left is what colour it is: cal-plain and cal-books draw the same twelve days, the same run and the same streak, and differ only in that one throws the book away. Days-only is a handsome tally that cannot say one word about what was read; coloured by books.cover_color it is a legible history — so reading_days.book_id should exist, which also gives the three dead tables (book_memos, book_compliments, poke_events, all empty in production) something to be about. Six flows are drawn end to end, including the night the reader nearly missed and the one they did. And state-sheet is the correction all of that needed. Versions 6–13 designed a nightly control, a bespoke position picker and a two-figure Library Card next to the shipped UI instead of out of it. The app already has showBookStatusBottomSheet — reached from the details page's edit_outlined action, and already “update this book's state”, with an AnimatedSize that reveals the dates each status has meaning for. So the position and the day become two more fields on that sheet, revealed at status == 1, under one Save — which deletes the “Today” chip, the whole +58/+32/+2 placement measurement, the separate wheel sheet, and the question of whether saving a position should stamp the day. The wheel is not a new control either: it is showSelectDateBottomSheet's sibling, the same 320pt sheet with a 92×32 Confirm and CupertinoPicker in place of the date picker. And the real Library Card is a hero StatTile carrying booksRead at 46pt over a board of covers, then tiles that have something true to say — so the streak is a peer of Pace, books read stays the hero, and the tile is omitted rather than zero-filled. Two things fall out: freezes are deferred (the month is redrawn without one, and the run falls from 12 to 3, which is the cost made visible), and the month grid has no home — the card is year-scoped, so a month pager would put two time scopes on one surface. The remaining open question is the cheap nightly path: the sheet is right for the truth and four taps too long for a habit. And the last group asks whether the progress row in the band should itself be the control — it is 333×30pt in the thumb's arc against the pencil's 48×48 in the top-right corner, over four times the area, for an act done ~30× a book. Three treatments: A wraps the row in the app's tap-to-edit field ground, C arms it in place with a thumb, −/+ and Done, and D opens the 320pt wheel. Drawing them corrected the prose four times over: A costs 27pt, not 14 (PROG_H 30 is the slot, so 44−30 double-counts the gap above the row); A only reaches the 44pt floor if it adopts DateFieldRow's 15pt body as well as its ground; copying that ground verbatim renders an invisible field, since the widget is surfaceVariant and so is the band (bar-a-naive keeps the trap on the record); and A's ground is already spoken for — the read-only period card 4pt above has the same white ground and the same radius, so the chevron is carrying the affordance in all three and A does not win by default if the chevron fails, it fails with them. And the group's own blind spot: A had only ever been drawn at rest, while C and D each had a rest frame and an opened one — so the one thing A proposed, a door, was the one thing never shown. Drawing it collapses the choice from three to two: DateFieldRow opens showSelectDateBottomSheet, so A's field opens the percent wheel — the same sheet D opens. A is not a third treatment, it is D plus a permanent 27pt tax, most of which the sheet then covers. The reader chose D, the bottom sheet — so C stays browsable as the rejected inline path, and the whole remaining question became what the row looks like at rest. Eight more treatments answer that (cl-*), and the winner reframes the problem: the rest state has two jobs, and every earlier attempt made one signal do both. “This is a door” is an interface question — so join the period card and the progress row into one grouped section, which turns the fatal adjacency into the mechanism, because a row in a list with a chevron is the one contract nobody has to learn. “You are here in this book” is a reading question — so end the fill in the ribbon every cover already carries, which is free. Merging is cheaper than bar-a, 22pt against 27, since the 10pt gap between the two cards buys a 1pt separator. Also drawn: the value as a small white button, which keeps the streak chip but costs 10pt to build a 27pt target; and a full-bleed row, free but 20pt out of alignment with everything else in the band. Two findings from looking: the bookmark only works white (solid brandText merged into its own fill and read as a blob), and two treatments pitched as free were not until measured. And the reader picked the band's own bottom edge as the look — linear, ends clipped by the 30pt radius. Which is where a correction belongs: on finding that a linear bar below ~8% sits entirely inside that radius (at p.13 of 320 it shows nothing), the bar was redrawn to trace the border, arcs included — and that is a different look, not a fix, so it was not a substitution to make. The traced frames stay browsable and are not the decision. The trade is now stated instead: full-bleed keeps the edge-to-edge look and goes blind below ~8%; spanning only the flat bottom (x30–x363) is visible at 1% and stops being an edge. Nothing sits in between, because the clipping is the look — and the blindness may well be fine, since the numbers sit directly above the bar, so an empty bar beside a legible 4% degrades rather than lies. DECIDED (pd-align): linear bar at the band's bottom edge, the progress row left alone on the grey band, the period card turned into a door, and the pencil retired. The card change is what earns the last of those: showBookStatusBottomSheet edits status, start and finish — exactly what that row displays — so once the row opens it, edit_outlined has no job and the app bar keeps only delete. The original complaint, a 48×48 target in the top-right dead zone, is solved twice over. But a chevron alone does not make the row look tappable, and the reason is grammatical: a list row reads label → value → chevron, and the period row packed its three pieces at the left so the chevron floated alone with a void between it and them. The fix is one rule — margin-left: auto on the value — giving the card DateFieldRow's shape verbatim, and it costs 2pt (the card draws 42, and the touch floor is 44). The pressed state is drawn too, being the half of “does it look tappable” that no resting frame can show — and it must not darken to the band's own surfaceVariant, or the card vanishes into it. Note what stops applying: bar-a failed because a white card here already meant “read-only”, and that card was the period card — once it is the door, there is no read-only white card left to confuse it with. All three frames leave the progress row untouched, which is asserted character-for-character rather than trusted. Written up as docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-12-reading-streaks-design.md.

A streak needs a daily signal and the schema has none. Books carry a start_date and a finish_date, memos and praise carry a created_at, and no row anywhere means “I read today”. Worse, the corpus shows the opposite behaviour: 168 of 293 finished books have start_date == finish_date, which is why bookReadingSpanDays has to treat a same-day pair as unknown — people catalogue, they do not track. So the proposal is not a button but a bookmark you move, paying for four things with one gesture: the daily signal, a progress percentage the app cannot currently show at all, a real reading span, and the first input the poke_events feed has ever had — its own migration defers the feed because there is “no activity to show”. Three anatomies, and the axis is resolution, computed rather than asserted: hanging from the chrome gives 2.5pt of travel per page but its tail crosses the tab strip and lies over the Notes list; on the cover, 0.56; across the fore-edge, 0.33, the truest metaphor and the only exactly proportional one, needing 32pt of headroom the hero's 20pt padding does not have. Drawing it caught the ribbon's tip landing at 53% of a cover the band beside it labelled 46% — two indicators of one fact disagreeing — and the 8pt nub is the fix. The streak's record is the due-date card from a library book's back pocket, not a flame, which is the visual language card-seal already established; a missed day is pale ink and never a red cross, because a second scolding is what makes people log days they did not read and put fiction into LibraryCardStats. Friends take Duolingo's unranked Friend Streaks pattern and explicitly reject its League: pages are not commensurable between two readers, so a pages leaderboard pays people to pick easier books, while days are. Open, and load-bearing: page_count is nullable and nobody has asked the corpus how often — if it is most books, the fallback button is the feature and the ribbon is a bonus, which inverts the design; a reader between books has no ribbon to move and loses a streak having done nothing wrong; a backwards drag has three readings and no chosen one; and the friend row's trailing slot is already spoken for by FriendReading.finishedCount.
Exact-page entry — the segment, the recovered wheel, the keypad nothing built · overrides the streaks spec's “no page/percent toggle” · design settled, 3 questions open · needs a provenance migration →
Follow-up to the row above, and a deliberate override of it. The streaks spec says “one native wheel … no page/percent toggle”, on the grounds that percent needs no fallback for the ~65% of books with no page_count. A segment offered only when page_count exists leaves exactly that majority untouched, so the objection does not defeat it — and the cost it was protecting is real: on a 432-page book the 101 stops reach 101 distinct pages, so p.200, p.201 and p.202 are unsayable, and p.200 round-trips to p.199. Storing page / pageCount as real needs no migration (exact for 320/432/912/1000). The control is the emphasised-centre wheel recovered from a89ce73 — the year/month filter sheet deleted in 604871f, centre 28/bold in brandText over 20/bold siblings with 0.5pt hairlines — given the tap-to-type keypad it never actually had. It cannot be copied verbatim: text_style_test.dart bans literal sizes in lib/ui, and only figure (30) is a mid-sized tabular token, so a wheel built from titleUser would wobble as it counts. Drawn at 1pt = 1px, so the rendered geometry is the claim: 320 = 64+220+36, seg-under 368, keypad-in-place 416, all under the 474.75 non-scroll-controlled ceiling — while keypad-below (580) and the system keyboard (548) provably do not fit. Two findings the drawing produced rather than confirmed: the header segment fits by 3pt (measured 129+8+110+8+92), which is the argument against it, and progressPage is p.{page} in English but {page}죕 in Korean — a prefix in one shipped locale and a suffix in the other — so the unit leaves the row and becomes a static column label. Also documents a live regression: Confirm rewrites selected/100 unconditionally, so merely opening the sheet and agreeing with it walks the bookmark back a page. The design is settled: the wheel's centred number is AppTextStyles.figure 30/w700 in brandText over subtitle 17/w600 siblings — chosen because figure is the only mid-sized tabular token and a non-tabular centre shifts sideways as it counts — at itemExtent 34, matching the date picker one tap away, with the recovered 0.5pt brandText hairlines and a bare numeral beside a static Page label. The segment gets its own row and Percent is always the default, on every book, so the sheet's opening state stays exactly today's; typing past the last page clamps, quietly and in secondaryText, which removes the error state entirely; and ProgressFieldRow prints a page only when a page count exists and the reader typed a page. That last one is the expensive one — it has to know how the value was entered, and the stored double cannot say. Inference by “is progress × 100 a whole number?” collapses exactly where books are commonest: counted over every page it is ambiguous for 0.9% of a 432-page book, 6.25% of a 320-page one, 50% of a 200-page one and 100% of a 50- or 100-page one — so provenance must be stored, and its shape (a boolean, or a nullable progress_page int recording what was actually typed) is the open question. The most bookish surface in the app is set in a UI token. BookVertical uses AppTextStyles.label, whose own doc says “tab labels, shelf tabs, badges, capsules, buttons” — so in the collapsed Library tab a book title is the same family and weight as the Library / Friends / Card labels 40pt below it, and it carries FontFeature.tabularFigures(), which is right for a count and wrong for a title. Three more findings, each measured off the drawing rather than asserted: 30 of the 110–124pt spine is padding, spent clearing an arch 5pt deep, so the measure is 80–94pt where it could be 93–107; half the truncation is not about measure at all but the parenthetical original title a Korean catalogue supplies as a matter of course (그로스 해킹(Growth Hacking) fits whole once cut at the bracket); and Center hides the per-book jitter this feature was built to add, because it puts every title at the middle of its own spine. And designhouseOTFLight.otf already ships, carries all 11,172 Hangul syllables, and is referenced nowhere — 376KB that never draws a glyph. Five layouts × twelve faces × nine sizes, switchable and independent — including five Korean serifs built as mockup-only subsets, among them Gowun Batang whole, so you can see what the app’s own serif would look like if it were not cut. Truncation is counted live off the drawing: 9 of 10 titles at now, 4 of 10 once the measure is fixed. Library Card — the cover row crop shipped bug · decided · built →
The cover row crops every jacket it draws, and the approved drawing could not show it. _tierFor hands out boxes at 0.36 and 0.50 where kDefaultCoverAspect is 0.67, and BoxFit.cover resolves the disagreement by centre-cropping: at twelve books each cover is 17pt wide in a 31pt jacket, so 20.7pt of artwork per cover is discarded, half off each side. The jitter makes it worse rather than softer — it perturbs width and height independently, so the aspect wanders between 0.30 and 0.49 and every cover is cropped by a different amount, which is why the row reads as a rendering fault. It shipped because artCoverRow in library-card-share fills each cover with a flat swatch, and a flat 0.36 rectangle reads as a spine. Ten options drawn against the real geometry, from a one-line alignment stopgap upward. Decided: shelf — two boards, three fixed sizes, and nothing squeezed. What boards and phase differed in turned out to be one choice rather than two designs — whether a book you have read is drawn as a cover or as a spine — so it was handed to the reader as a setting: a panel at the card’s top right offering Covers or Spines. Then it was taken back. The card draws overlapping covers and nothing else: a shelf of spines is a picture of a different object, and covers are the one asset a competitor cannot fake, so a setting that hides them is a setting that gives away the point. The withdrawn modes stay drawable there as the evidence, relabelled rather than deleted. A cover is 52.8pt, a spine is 16.6pt (BookVertical’s own proportion at that height), and a leaning cover advances by 16.6pt — equal to the spine width, because a leaning book shows exactly a spine’s worth of itself. Currently-reading books sit at the front as whole covers with a bookmark and no month stamp. Past capacity nothing thins or crowds; the shelf prints how many it is not showing — so capacity becomes a fact rather than something to engineer: 31 covers, 26 with two books open, 24 with three — three fewer than before the seal’s corner was held, with the withdrawn modes measured beside them at 9 and 31. Three is also as many books as can be open at the front, because the top board must still be able to draw a finished one once the seal is held: that is kCardReadingMax, and it was two until the rule stopped being a fraction of the board and became the guarantee. Two boards is also the last layout the well permits (a third leaves 30.7pt of cover against a 51.9pt floor). Resolved: the bookmark carries the distinction and the figure does not move — the hero keeps counting finished books, so a card with two open books prints 22 over 24 covers, and the two wearing a bookmark are visibly a different class of thing. A figure counting both would make “22 books” mean “20 finished and 2 started”, which is a worse number and a claim library_card_stats.dart does not make.
