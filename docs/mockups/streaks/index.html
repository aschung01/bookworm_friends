Reading streaks — the bookmark, the card, the list

Corrections, found after these were drawn — three of them, and all three change the design.

1. The app already has a bookmark, and it is white. ReadingBookmark in lib/ui/widgets/book/reading_bookmark.dart ships today: bookmarkIcon.svg, a 22×38 box holding a 13.5×30 white notched ribbon, inset kReadingBookmarkInset 8 from the cover's right edge, its shadow dropped 4 and blurred 4 at half strength. It is drawn on shelf rows and on the Library Card, and it already means something: this is the book you have open. Every ribbon below is an invented brand-green one, 18pt wide, 74pt across the cover — precisely the mistake pitfalls.md records as having cost the library set three review rounds. These need redrawing against the real asset, and the design question changes with them: may the mark that means open also mean page 148, or is that two jobs for one object? Its doc comment is worth reading first — it was two marks once, and was deliberately made one.

2. page_count is 35% covered, and structurally so. 20260816160000_book_page_count.sql measured that on the migrated corpus, and backfill_book_page_count.py says why it will not improve: Kakao, the only provider that actually knows this corpus, has no page field at all — eleven keys, none of them pages — and Google Books found 16 of 40 ISBNs where Kakao found 40 of 40. So about two books in three arrive with no total. The answer is not a fallback button: store position as a fraction and let the page number be a display layer. A fraction needs no total, so the same control serves every book and the read-out becomes a percentage — which is what an ebook reader is already looking at. Asking for the count once when a book is started is then an upgrade rather than a gate.

3. The rest of the crop was audited against the code, and three more things had moved. The status badge is not in the hero's corner — reading_period_row.dart says it used to sit there on a friend's book and at the top of the right column on your own, “two places for one thing”, and it was deliberately moved into the reading-period row where one rule serves both viewers. Drawing it in the corner, as this page did, reproduced a design that was explicitly retired. The corner holds the shelf label alone, with its count (소설 12) — the count is part of the hero contract, not decoration, because the label flies between screens and must not resize in the air. And the period card is real chrome: a white surface card at radius 10 carrying the badge, the date range and the day count in brandText, which drops to a bare badge when there is no start date. The app bar was wrong too and is fixed: surfaceVariant at Material's default 56pt, a 44pt back disc, and delete_outline + edit_outlined at 24pt — present only when isSelf.

Nothing here is built and nothing is decided. A streak needs a daily signal and this app has none: the schema records books, memos, praise and pokes, but no row anywhere means “I read today”. These drawings began from the proposal that the signal is a bookmark you move, so one gesture feeds the streak, the progress percentage, a real reading span and the dormant activity feed at once — and ended somewhere much narrower: the bookmark shows, and the signal is one tap on the book, with no number in it. Drawn at 1px = 1pt on a 393pt frame, cropped to the book-details hero, the same unit the praise-region set uses. Colours are AppColors.light verbatim — surfaceVariant #E9ECEF for the hero, brandFill #067657 for the ribbon body and the page tag, brand #09BC8A for its lit edge only, because app_theme.dart documents that token as decoration at 2.45:1 and the tag carries text. The cover is 120×180 from kDefaultCoverAspect 2/3, the shelf 8pt with offset(0,2) blur 2 black@25%, the badge h10/v4 radius 10. One version, drawn after fourteen. The exploration is collapsed: the chain is still applied to build these frames, so every figure stays derived, but only the answer is browsable. The axis moved nine times to get here. First it was not where the mark sits but what a page maps onto — the mark's length or its position. Length has a floor at the asset's own 43.6pt, so part of every book cannot be drawn at all; position has no floor. Then reading-lamp moved it off the input question entirely: the field pattern (Fable, Goodreads, Life Reset) is that the mark displays and a sheet asks. Then one-tap moved it to how many decisions the nightly path contains, and its answer was none. Then tap-target moved it to which gesture stamps the day, given that a tap already means “open the book” on every other shelf — five candidates, and the one that invents no gesture won. stamp-a moved it to where that button lives on a screen whose chrome is already full, which turned out to be a question about points: 58 for a full-width button, 32 for a wrapped chip, 2 for a one-word one. And streak-home asked the thing none of them had: where the reader sees their own number. It had a home from the first draft — the Library Card — but the library screen carried only states, so the count is now drawn in every slot that could hold it. And streak-counter draws the streak the way Duolingo actually builds one — six moments, not a number — and settles the wheel on the way past: 912 page-stops is about 91 flicks, 101 percent-stops is ten, so the wheel spins a fraction — the unit the app must learn to store and the page becomes a derived label. Finally streak-flows moved the axis to what a recorded day has to remember, and answered it by drawing the month twice: the reference's calendar joins consecutive days into one continuous capsule rather than separate marks, and once the run is a single object the only question left is what colour it is. Days-only is a tally; coloured by book it is a history — so reading_days.book_id should exist. Then state-sheet moved the axis one last time, to which existing surface owns this — and the answer deleted a great deal of the above. The details page's edit_outlined action already opens showBookStatusBottomSheet, which is already “update this book's state” and already reveals fields conditionally on status. So the position and the day are two more rows on that sheet, the wheel is the date picker's sibling, and the streak is a StatTile on the real Library Card rather than a second hero figure. All figures are computed in SECTION 2 or measured off the rendered frame, so no drawing can disagree with them — see el-deadzone. Switch version with v, compare two with ⇧Enter.
