Tapping a friend: three mechanics

Each shown as tap → mid-transition → settled. Locked in: Activity keeps its name, glass popover for the collapsed filter, month filtering dropped, Add Friend icon-only, rail holds friends only, horizontal swipe pages between friends.

The question underneath: the Friends sheet and a friend’s Books-read sheet are different content competing for one slot. Each mechanic below answers “where did Friends go?” differently — and that answer, not the animation, is what you’re choosing.

Tab bar stays live, world persists, and the sheet dropping reads as “here she is.” Swipe the background left or right to page to the next friend, and the rail selection follows. Three things move at once though — background cross-fade, rail descending, sheet content swapping mid-fall.
★ My recommendation
With your own avatar out of the rail, C has exactly one free way back. Tapping the already-active Friends tab clears the selection — standard iOS pop-to-root, nothing to learn, no pixels spent. It is now load-bearing rather than a nicety: without it the Everyone list is unreachable after your first pick. If that feels too hidden, the alternative is a back chevron in the bar to the left of “jisoo’s Library”, which costs a little width but is unmissable. B doesn’t have this problem at all — swipe-to-dismiss is the way back.

Swipe paging is confirmed, and it prices the mechanics differently. In C the background owns a horizontal PageView while the sheet owns a vertical drag, so both gestures live on screen at once and arbitration is real work — your _DelayedReorderableListener already exists because of a milder version of this. In B the pushed page owns paging outright and the sheet inside it is short, which is the cheapest place to put it. In A paging would have to happen inside a draggable sheet, alongside per-shelf horizontal scroll: three nested directions, and the reason to rule A out even before the duplication argument.
