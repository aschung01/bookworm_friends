What “Read” looks like with the whole canvas

All three show the Read state of the toggle. Floating glass controls sit top-right, Flighty-style: the Reading / Read switch, and the date filter under it.

The switch costs nothing at the data layer — withoutFinishedBooks() already splits these two sets, and finished books keep their shelf_id. What differs below is purely how the read set is grouped, and that’s the whole decision.

1 — Same shelves, status swapped

The shelf structure is the constant; status is the filter. Flip the toggle and your shelves stay exactly where they are — the books on them change. The read copy of “Novels” sits where the unread one did.

Nearly free: withoutFinishedBooks() gains a mirror, onlyFinishedBooks(), and the same _ShelfRow renders both. Weakness: no sense of time or accumulation — a year of reading looks identical to a decade of it.

2 — Month shelves

Shelf labels become months. A year of reading renders as a bookcase you fill up — uneven shelves are the story, and a thin month is visible at a glance. This is the passport-stamp feeling, and it’s what the date filter was always implying.

Cheap: group the same rows by finish_date instead of shelf_id. Pairs naturally with the year filter, and it’s the visual twin of the Library Card — shelf as picture, card as number. Trade-off: your shelf names disappear in this view.

3 — Cover grid by month

Full covers instead of spines, four across, month headers between. By far the most legible — you can actually recognise a book — and it scales to hundreds without turning into a smear of 7px slivers.

Most new UI: a grid view that doesn’t reuse _ShelfRow at all. And it abandons the shelf metaphor at exactly the moment you’re showing off, which is the moment it was earning the most.

Note the toggle also retires FinishedBooksSheet, freeing the bottom of the screen that the floating tab bar now wants.
