Three contents, all drawn in the shape C shell (sheet over a live shelf wall).

Worth naming: this choice is mostly independent of A / B / C. Nav shape decides the chrome — in A these become opaque full pages, in B they sit in a dimmed sheet. 1 — Friend swiper

Today’s avatar bar and PageView move here wholesale. The sheet is deliberately short because the friend’s shelf is the content — it fills the world behind. Scroll the rail, the wall behind changes owner.

Reuses everything you already have: _FriendAvatarBar, _selectedFriendProvider, userLibraryProvider. Zero new queries, zero schema. Also simplifies My Library’s header, which loses the avatar bar.

2 — Activity feed

Flighty’s literal model. Chronological rows of what people you follow are doing. Highest pull-to-open value, and the only option that gives you a reason to open the app when nothing of yours changed.

Needs a new feed query joining follows × books × book_compliments. Two catches: nothing records "started"/"added" as an event — you’d infer them from start_date and created_at, so edits rewrite history. And pokes aren’t stored at all — poke_user() only fires a push, so pokes can’t appear here without a new table.

3 — Hybrid: rail + feed

Avatar rail pinned to the top of the sheet, feed below it. Tapping an avatar filters the feed and swaps the wall behind to that friend’s shelf, so dragging the sheet down is how you visit them.

Everything option 2 needs, plus the rail wiring and a filtered variant of the feed query. Strictly the most work — and the only one where both halves of the tab compete for the same vertical space.
