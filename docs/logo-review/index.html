AI moodboard — round 1 reference only

Not shippable. All six baked a rounded-rect icon shape into the artwork on a white outer background — so under a real platform mask you get a rounded rect inside a rounded rect. Switch Mask to Squircle to see it. Three of six also broke the palette. Useful for the questions they raise, not as candidates.

AI moodboard — round 2: hand-drawn & isometric reference only

Prompts fixed to demand a full-bleed background and heavier stroke weight. That worked — 7 of 8 dropped the frame and the palette held on all 8. The 48 px column is the point of this section: filled mass survives, outline does not. Every hand-drawn style is outline-based, which is why they all dissolve. Not fixable by thickening — inherent to the style.

AI moodboard — round 3: brush, linocut & chalk reference only

Eight closer variations based specifically on H1, H2 and H4. Four test filled handmade masses; four test alternate compositions and print textures. The useful result: roughness survives only when it lives on the edge of a large shape. Fine grain inside the mark still turns grey at 48 px.

Eight variations on H7, changing composition rather than just texture. The useful split is now visible: outlined chalk feels warmer, but a filled chalk mass with broad negative-space cuts survives small sizes substantially better.

AI mood

board — round 5: H7 refinements, three books reference only

Redone properly. These are image-to-image edits of H7 itself, not fresh generations from a text description, so the chalk medium and warmth carry over instead of being re-invented. Every one keeps exactly three books, and each changes a single variable. H7 is included first for direct comparison.

Round 6: K4 fill correction reference only

K4 left green holes in books two and three, where each cover continues underneath the book resting on it. Three edits of K4, all filling those regions so every cover is a solid white slab with only a thin green separation gap between books. Original K4 shown first.

Round 7: simpler reference only

“Simpler” has two readings, so both are here. M1–M6 keep the chalk and strip content away — fewer books, no bookmark, calmer alignment. N1–N2 keep the composition and drop the chalk for flat vector shapes. M6 and both N marks also fix the white-ribbon-on-white-cover problem the shipped icon still has, by cutting the bookmark in as a green notch instead of laying it on top.

Round 8: simpler still reference only

Eight hand-authored flat marks that each reduce N1/N2 along a different axis, rather than eight versions of one idea. Shape count runs from three down to one. O1 and O6 are deliberate floors — included to find where reduction stops paying, and both do. O2 drops the bookmark and lets width taper carry the stack; O4 inverts the construction to one white mass with green rules cut through it; O5 and O7 break out of the horizontal-bar family every other candidate belongs to.

Round 9: the single book reference only

Taking M4 seriously. M4 had three fixable faults — it sampled #27B98C rather than brand #09BC8A, its art filled only 47.5% × 61.3% of the canvas against the ~76% target, and its ribbon was white on a white cover, held together by a chalk hairline. P1 fixes all three and changes nothing else, so the idea can be judged on its merits; P2–P7 then vary the one book by detail, orientation and form. M4 itself is shown last for comparison.

The round also turned up the answer to the obvious objection. A single book has no stack in it, and the app is called Libstack — but drawing P1 showed that a back board peeking out from behind a cover is visually indistinguishable from a second book. M4 was never really one book. P8 runs that device once more and gets the pile back without giving up M4's character.
